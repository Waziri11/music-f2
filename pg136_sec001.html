(c) Audio technician or sound engineer:

An audio technician or sound engineer works to make sure that the sound quality is clear and balanced. They operate studio equipment during recordings, do sound checks before sessions and mix and edit the audio after recording. Their role is very important in producing music that sounds professional.

(d) Lyricist:

A lyricist writes the words (lyrics) to songs. Sometimes, they work alone, and other times they work with musicians or composers. Lyricists help to create meaningful messages in songs by choosing the right words that match the mood and rhythm of the music.

(e) A music production teacher:

A music production teacher is someone who teaches students how to create, record and produce music. They may work in schools, colleges or universities. This job requires good knowledge of music, studio equipment and teaching skills to help learners understand the music production process.

(f) Sound designer:

A sound designer creates sound effects and audio features for different types of media. These include video games, films, theatre, radio shows and adverts. Sound designers use computers and special equipment to make sure the sound supports the story or message being told.

(g) Songwriter:

A songwriter is someone who writes songs, often for other people to sing. Many famous songs have been performed by singers who did not write them. In Tanzania, for example, the national anthem and patriotic songs like “Tanzania Nakupenda,” and “Tazama Ramani” have been recorded by many artists who are not the original composers. Songwriting is a creative job that can bring income through music studios, performances and royalties.

(h) Instrumentalists:

An instrumentalist is someone who plays a musical instrument. They can record instrumental music and sell it to singers or producers. Instrumentalists are also hired to play their instruments during studio recording sessions. Their work brings richness to recorded songs.
