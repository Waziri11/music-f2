Chapter One

Rhythm

Introduction

Without precise rhythm, music lacks order, leaving nothing to dance to or nod along with. While other elements like pitch, dynamics and harmony are essential, it is impossible to have music without rhythm. In this chapter, you will learn several concepts related to rhythm, which include triplets, time signatures, and grouping of notes and rests. The competencies developed will enable you to read and write rhythmic patterns accurately and perform music in different meters.

Triplets

When a note that is commonly divided into two equal parts is divided into three equal parts, the resulting group is known as a triplet. For example, one semibreve can be divided into three equal parts of minims. A minim can be divided into three equal parts of crotchets. A crotchet can also be divided into three equal parts of quavers. Table 1.1 shows how a beat that is usually divided into two is divided into three equal parts. Note that, when forming a triplet, notes with flags like quavers, semiquavers and other smaller notes are beamed together, with the number “3” placed in the middle of the notes. Notes without flags, like crotchets and minims, are grouped by a straight or curvy line with a “3” in the middle.
